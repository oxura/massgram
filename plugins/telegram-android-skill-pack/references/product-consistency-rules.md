# Telegram Android Product Consistency Rules

## Core Product Posture

- Be useful before being expressive.
- Reduce friction before adding surface area.
- Respect the user's pace.
- Keep messaging and communication clarity primary.

## Strong Defaults

- Favor direct actions and obvious controls.
- Favor one clear way to complete the task.
- Favor remembered choices over repeated prompts.
- Favor small integrated features over separate experiences.

## Reject These Product Smells

- Engagement gimmicks, streaks, gamification, or social pressure loops
- Unnecessary onboarding, education, or confirmation steps
- Promotional empty states or decorative explanation screens
- Features that compete with chat, composer, or navigation clarity
- New destinations created mainly to showcase the feature

## Placement Rules

- Put infrequent or advanced controls in existing menus, sheets, or settings sections.
- Put fast repeated actions close to where the user already acts.
- Do not promote a feature to primary chrome unless it is central to messaging behavior.

## Decision Questions

- Is the feature obviously useful?
- Is it faster than the current workflow?
- Would a Telegram power user understand it without training?
- Does it stay out of the way when unused?
- Does it still feel normal in a messaging-first product?

## Decision Output

- Approve when the idea is useful, direct, and naturally integrated.
- Narrow when the idea is valid but the proposed surface is too loud.
- Reject when the idea adds ceremony, novelty, or engagement mechanics.
