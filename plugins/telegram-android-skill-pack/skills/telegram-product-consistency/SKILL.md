# Telegram Product Consistency

## Ask the core question first

- Ask, "Would this feel normal inside Telegram?"
- If the answer is uncertain, narrow the proposal until it fits existing Telegram expectations.
- Protect speed, directness, and low-friction repeat use over novelty or delight.

## Apply the product rules

- Favor utility-first features over engagement-first features.
- Favor direct manipulation over guided flows and explanatory ceremony.
- Favor obvious actions over clever multi-step interactions.
- Favor integrating into existing menus, rows, and flows over launching new top-level destinations.
- Favor remembered choices and sane defaults over repeated confirmations.

## Reject the wrong product instincts

- Reject fake social mechanics, streaks, dopamine loops, and manipulative prompts.
- Reject unnecessary onboarding, walkthroughs, and modal education for straightforward features.
- Reject features that clutter chat, composer, or navigation chrome without paying their rent.
- Reject "premium-looking" UX additions that slow down power users.

## Produce a clear decision

- Approve the idea when it is useful, direct, and naturally integrated.
- Narrow the idea when the utility is real but the proposed surface is too loud or too broad.
- Reject the idea when it damages messaging clarity, adds gimmicks, or needs too much explanation.
- Suggest the smallest Telegram-native version of the idea when the original request overshoots.
